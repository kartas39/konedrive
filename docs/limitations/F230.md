- **F230. A fill's file calls run in blocking sections, and a section that has begun ends by itself**
  (`hydration/source/target.rs`, `hydration/source/fill.rs`, `hydration/source/parts.rs`,
  `folder/locks.rs` `holding`; quality findings `HY4`, `X2`) — the fill makes no `pwrite`,
  `fsync`, `fallocate` or attribute call on a runtime thread: each run of calls that used to
  follow each other without a wait is one section on a blocking thread. A section cannot be
  stopped once it has begun. What that leaves:
  - **A fill dropped during a section is over for its caller before the section is.** A fill
    is dropped when OneDrive removes its item (`unless_removed`, #104) or when the caller of
    `Hydrate` goes away. The section under way ends on its thread, and the per-inode lock stays
    taken until it has (`InodeHold`). When the section is the commit, the file ends `hydrated`
    and complete, while the opener was answered `EIO` (or the pin's download counted as failed)
    and the activity log says "removed in OneDrive". Before, a drop came before the commit or
    not at all. The file is correct either way.
  - **The lock keeps off only what takes the lock**: another fill, a free-up, a replacement, an
    upload, the examination, startup recovery. A removal that succeeds does not take it:
    `forget_before_removing` cancels the fill, and `remove_whole`, `kept_by_resync` and
    `release_other_names` then read the state, strip attributes and unlink while the section
    may still be running. That was so before as well: a run of calls under way ended beside the
    materializer, which is on a thread of its own. Only `settle_stopped`, after a removal that
    *failed*, waits for the lock (10 s in all, `SETTLE_WAIT`,
    `remote/materialize/rw/removal.rs`), and so for the section; an `fsync` slower than what is
    left of that leaves the file as it is, with the warning that was there for a fill that did
    not let go in time.
  - **A section left behind keeps the lock table's row** (`Held` owns it) for as long as it
    runs, after its fill is gone:
    - a removal that arrives then, the fill having been dropped for another reason (the caller
      of `Hydrate` gone), gets `true` from `InodeLocks::cancel`, logs "a download into a file
      being removed is stopped" at `info` and counts the file among those it stopped
      (`survey.stopped`), where there was no row and no line before;
    - startup recovery's `try_lock` passes over such a file ("being filled or freed up right
      now") for the length of the section, and the other `try_lock` callers come back later;
    - the event descriptor stays open in the daemon until the section ends, after
      `hydrate_done(EIO)` has gone to the helper.
  - **Only a fill started under the lock keeps it**: the fills of `hydration/server.rs`,
    `sync/hydrate.rs` (through `unless_removed`) and `upload/move_out.rs` (`holding`). A fill
    with no lock (a file whose identity could not be read; the tests) has none to keep. The
    hold reaches the fill through a task-local; a new caller that takes the lock and fills has
    to run the fill inside `holding`, on the same task, and nothing makes it.
  - **A fill dropped while a section runs does not start the next one.** Where two runs of
    calls followed each other without a wait, a drop between them now ends the fill there:
    - a write that fails, and the roll-back;
    - the last write, and the commit;
    - the second hash mismatch: `remove_progress`, and the roll-back;
    - a start-over: `remove_progress`, and the next fetch (a wait already);
    - in parts, the `End::Mismatch` and `End::DropCheckpoint` arms of `parts::download`:
      `remove_progress`, and the next attempt or the roll-back;
    - the start (`begin`) and the continuation point, when nothing is to be cleared.

    The file is left `hydrating` with what was written, which is what a drop during the
    download always left: the next open or startup recovery takes it from there. One thing
    differs for the user: after a write that failed on a full disk and no roll-back, the
    partial blocks stay allocated until that next open or recovery.
  - **In a download in parts a checkpoint excludes the writes** (it waits for those under way,
    and none starts until it ends), as the `fsync` under the attempt's mutex did; the writes of
    different streams run beside each other.
  - **Left on the runtime thread**, as calls that do not wait on a disk: the two `fstat`s on the
    event descriptor and the `readlink` of `/proc/self/fd/<n>` in `hydration/server.rs` (the
    size for the pool, the identity for the lock, the name to show); the hashing of each buffer
    (`QuickXor::update`), which is computing, not file I/O. `back_to_placeholder` is still a
    plain function: its caller is the materializer, which is not on a runtime thread.
  - **`download_into` duplicates the descriptor it is given** (a section may outlive the
    caller's borrow); a `dup` that fails (`EMFILE`) fails the download with that errno, clamped.
  - **A section the runtime does not run** (it is shutting down) panics the fill, like a panic
    inside the section; `hydration/server.rs` denies that open with `EIO`.

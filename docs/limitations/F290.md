- **F290. After a panic under a lock, the next user of the lock goes on with the data as the panic left it**
  (`konedrived/src/panic.rs` `lock`, `read`, `write`; `konedrive-graph/src/lib.rs` `lock`;
  `konedrive-tree/src/outbox/changes.rs` `dirty`; quality finding `X4`) — every lock of
  `std::sync` in the daemon, in `konedrive-graph` and in `konedrive-tree` is taken whether or
  not a holder of it panicked: 145
  sites. Before, 98 of them panicked in turn (the other 47 wrote the recovery out), so one panic
  under such a lock took every later user of it down too: a D-Bus call, a sync's cycle, the daemon's
  stop. Nothing checks that the data is whole; it is kept whole by what is done under each lock:
  - **Most locks guard one value or one collection**, and each change of it is one assignment,
    one insert, one remove or one take. A panic cannot be between two steps.
  - **Five run code they are handed, or another module's, while the lock is held**, and a panic in that code leaves
    what is listed here:
    - `local/watcher/mod.rs` `Shared::update` (the watcher's `status`): the closure it is handed
      runs under the lock. Each of the eleven closures only assigns or counts up plain fields
      of `WatchStatus` with no rule between them, and `note` only reads them, so what was set
      stays set and nothing is half-written. The status hook (`on_status`) runs after the lock
      is let go. The guards that end a thread (`Ending` of the reader, `ExaminerEnding`) run
      while a panic unwinds and read and write the status then, as before.
    - `sync/pause.rs` `show`: `publish` panics, so the pause shown is not remembered and a timed
      pause gets no timer. The next `show` starts it; until then a timed pause does not end by
      itself. `forget` stops the timer and forgets the pause before it calls `publish`.
    - `conditions/running.rs` `change`: the settings are plain values with no rule between
      them; what the closure had set stays set.
    - `daemon/manager.rs` `list`: the registry is told first and the account listed after, and
      the registry adds the account as its last step, so the account is in both lists or in
      neither. `unlist` takes the account off the manager's list first and off the registry's
      after; what the registry does there is two removals, which cannot panic.
    - `hydration/pin.rs`, every section that publishes the count or the waiting size
      (`state.update`): the queue and the set were changed before the publish, so they are
      whole and the published figure is the one before. The next change publishes again, and a
      sweep counts everything again from the folder.
  - The other two of `local/` are of the first kind. The watcher's `flushes` (the senders that
    wait for a flush) is pushed to, taken whole or cleared, one step each; the reader's `Ending`
    clears it, so a `flush` that comes after the reader ended, panic or not, pushes its sender,
    sees `reader_done`, clears and answers `false`. Before, a `flush` after a panic under that
    lock panicked itself. The ignore list is cloned under its read lock in
    `local/watcher/service.rs`; its one writer (`sync/settings.rs`) writes `config.toml` and then replaces the whole list
    in one assignment, so a reader finds the old list or the new one.
  - **Two locks guard something outside themselves** and hold no data: `account/mod.rs`
    `cache_lock` (one read-modify-write of `account.json`, written by a rename, so the file is
    the old one or the new one) and `helper/hub.rs` `publishing` (the link is set and
    `HelperState` published; a panic between them leaves the state of before, and the wake
    that follows the publish is not sent. `check` works the state out from the link again,
    but while there is a link nothing asks for one until the link changes: until then
    `HelperState` can say `unknown` beside a live link).
  - `config/store.rs` keeps its own word "poisoned" for a `config.toml` that could not be read.
    It is not about a panic, and nothing of it changed.

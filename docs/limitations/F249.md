- **F249. The order of an account's locks is a written rule, and two of them are held across slow work**
  (`konedrived/src/account/mod.rs`, the module's "The order of the locks"; `account/sign_in.rs`,
  `account/mode.rs`; quality findings `AC6`, `X2`) — FRAGILE, *reasoned*, open.
  - **Nothing but the text keeps the order.** `session`, then the token manager's refresh lock,
    then the leaves (`ConfigStore`'s lock, `cache_lock`, the state, `uploads`, `siblings`, the
    token manager's own), none of the leaves held while another is asked for; no account's
    `session` held while another account is asked. The compiler does not see it: whoever adds
    a call of `session.lock()` to what a refresh calls back (`note_granted`), to what
    `commit_as` runs or to what the folder does when the quota was read
    (`PendingUploads::quota_read`, called under `session`), or reads `config.toml` or
    `account.json` inside a `StateHandle::update`, or asks a sibling account under `session`,
    makes a deadlock that no test here would find.
  - **`session` is held across the wallet.** A commit of a sign-in, a commit of the switch to
    read-write and a sign-out store or delete the refresh token under it, and the wallet may
    show its unlock prompt then: until the person answers, a cancel, a sign-out, a switch to
    read-only and the account-info refresh of that account wait. `begin_sign_in` and the switch
    to read-write refuse what they can before they ask for the lock, so those two answer at
    once. It is held on purpose: the check of an attempt and the storing of its token are one
    step.
  - **The refresh lock is held across `fsync`ed writes on a runtime thread.** The commit of the
    switch to read-write writes `account.json` (twice) and `config.toml` under it
    (`commit_read_write`, in `TokenManager::commit_as`), and every refresh whose grant changed
    writes `account.json` (`note_granted`). On a slow disk every caller that wants a token of
    that account waits that long, and the runtime thread is taken meanwhile. They are not
    moved to a blocking thread: `commit_as` runs its work under the lock in one piece, so that
    no refresh records a narrower grant in between, and a section on another thread would
    outlive a caller that is dropped (F231 has the readers that stay on runtime threads).
